# Storekeeper guide

You receive goods, move stock between stores and count it. Everything is in the back office (web browser) under **Stock** and **Purchasing**.

## Receiving a delivery

1. **Purchasing → Goods received → New**, choose the purchase order (or the supplier when there is none).
2. Enter what **actually arrived** — count it, do not copy the delivery note. Batch numbers and expiry dates where the product asks for them.
3. **Post**. The stock goes up and the cost is recorded. A posted receipt cannot be changed: a mistake is corrected by reversing it and receiving again.

**Why:** the receipt is what the supplier is paid against, and what the shelves are measured against.

## Moving stock

**Stock → Transfers → New**: from which store, to which store, the items. The sending store **dispatches**, the receiving store **receives** what really arrived. Differences show on the transfer.

**Asking another branch:** **Stock → Stock requests → Ask for stock**: your warehouse, the branch to ask, and the items as your branch sells them. When the other branch sends it, a transfer appears for you to **receive**. Asking a branch of another industry (for example a pharmacy asking the grocery) also needs your manager's approval and theirs; the request shows who it is waiting for.

**When a branch asks you:** the number on *Stock requests* shows requests waiting. Open one, click **Send**, and enter what you can spare (0 for none), or **Reject** it with a reason. For a request from another industry, choose your own product for each item.

**Grocery stores, every morning:** **Grocery → Markdowns**: apply the suggested markdowns, print and stick the stickers, and write off expired packs.

## Waybills (goods on the move)

**Stock → Waybills → New waybill**, or the **Waybill** button on a stock transfer, a customer order that has been delivered, or a return to a supplier.

1. Choose what it is for and the document; the goods are copied from it. Add the transport company, driver, vehicle, packages and weight.
2. **Print waybill** and send it with the goods. **Dispatch** when they leave (driver and vehicle).
3. When they arrive, the receiver **scans the QR code** on the waybill with their phone, types their name (and can add a photo) and confirms; or bring back the signed copy and click **Mark delivered** (upload a photo of it).

On goods received from a supplier, **Supplier waybill** records their waybill number, transporter and vehicle, with a scan.

**Why:** a waybill proves what left, who carried it and who received it; disputes about missing items are settled with the signed copy or the receiver's confirmation.

## Counting

1. **Stock → Stock counts → New** for the store (all products, or one category). Print the **count sheet**.
2. Count what is on the shelf. Do **not** look at the system's figure while counting.
3. Type the counts in (or import the sheet: **Import counts**). **Submit** for approval.
4. A manager approves; the differences become stock movements with their value.

## Expired and near-expiry stock

**Stock → Expiry & returns**: tick the expired batches (one store at a time), then **Dispose** (method and witness) or **Return to supplier**. Put the packs aside marked "not for sale"; a second person approves before the stock leaves the system. Print the disposal record or the return note for signatures.

## Adjusting stock (damage, expiry, theft)

**Stock → Stock adjustments → New**, reason, items, quantities. Above the limit, another manager approves. Never adjust to "make the count right" without a count.

## Practise first

In the **practice company**: receive a purchase order with one item short, transfer 5 cartons between stores, and count one category with two differences.
